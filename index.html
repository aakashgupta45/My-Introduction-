A small country, an enormous view

Eight of the world's fourteen highest peaks rise from a strip of land smaller than most countries you can name.

Nepal runs from tropical lowland to the roof of the world in under 200 kilometers — jungle, terraced hillside, alpine forest, and glacier, stacked on top of each other. It is the birthplace of the Buddha, home to living goddesses and centuries-old pagoda temples, and to more than a hundred languages spoken across its hills.

Where to begin

Five places that hold the country together

Kathmandu Valley

Three former royal cities — Kathmandu, Patan, Bhaktapur — packed with carved-wood courtyards and pagoda temples still in daily use.

827m

Pokhara

A lake town facing the Annapurna range head-on, and the usual starting point for treks into the Himalaya.

3,440m+

Khumbu (Everest region)

Sherpa villages, monasteries, and suspension bridges strung along the trail toward Everest Base Camp.

Chitwan

Lowland jungle and grassland along the Rapti River, home to one-horned rhinos and the Tharu communities who live beside them.

150m

Lumbini

A quiet pilgrimage garden marking the birthplace of the Buddha, with monasteries built by nations across Asia.

The calendar

A country that celebrates often

DashainAshwin / October
The longest and largest festival, when families gather across generations and elders bless the young with tika and jamara.
TiharKartik / November
Five days honoring crows, dogs, cows, and finally the bond between brothers and sisters, marked by oil lamps and rangoli.
HoliFalgun / March
The festival of color, when streets and courtyards turn into an open canvas of powder and water.
Indra JatraBhadra / September
Kathmandu's own street festival — masked dances and the chariot procession of the living goddess, Kumari.
